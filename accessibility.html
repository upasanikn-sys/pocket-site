Accessibility

Checked before every publish · Written 29 October 2026 · Updated 3 November 2026 · Applies to this website
In short: this website aims to meet WCAG 2.2 at level AA. Before any change is published, Pocket's own test tools check every page, in light and dark and at phone and desktop sizes, against the standards below, and nothing ships while one fails. The checks are automated. The authors have also run one automated pass with the NVDA screen reader, which is not a test by people who use one. Nobody who uses a screen reader every day has tested the site yet; that is the next step, and the limits below say what is not yet known.

1. What every page is held to

  • Contrast. Text stands at least 4.5:1 against what is behind it, and at least 5:1 when it is 14px or smaller. No text is smaller than 12px, in a drawing either.
  • Room to press. Every button and control is at least 24 by 24 pixels; a link inside a sentence takes the size of its text.
  • Keyboard. Each page starts with a link that skips to its content. On the inner pages a keyboard walk reaches every link and control with Tab and finds a visible ring on each.
  • Names. Every control has a name a screen reader can read out, and on the inner pages every figure does too.
  • Zoom and narrow screens. No page scrolls sideways at any width from 320 to 1728 pixels, in light or dark.
  • High contrast. With Windows' contrast themes on, no mark drawn in colour disappears.
  • Motion. With Reduce motion on, nothing animates: every change happens at once and every figure shows without a transition.
  • Automated rules. axe-core finds no serious or critical problem on any page.
  • Film. The product film has captions, and it plays only when you press play.

2. Every criterion, one row each

WCAG 2.2 has 55 criteria at levels A and AA. Each has a row: what the website does about it, and what checks that. A row says Held, by a script only when it names a test tool that exists in Pocket's own repository, and these rows are read back against the standard before every publish. It says Held, by hand when the site is built that way and a person has looked, but no script checks it. This is not a claim of conformance: that is for people who rely on assistive technology to say, and none has yet.

Of the 55 criteria at levels A and AA in WCAG 2.2: 24 held and checked by a script, 21 held and checked by hand, 9 do not apply to a site like this, 1 not yet known. A script is one of Pocket's own test tools; a person who uses a screen reader every day has not yet checked any of it. The authors' one NVDA pass (3 November 2026) was a script pressing keys, not such a person; what it covered is below.

3. The one keyboard shortcut

Pressing / anywhere on the site opens search, as it does on many sites. It does nothing while you are typing in a field. Speech-input and some switch users find a one-key shortcut can fire by mistake, so it can be turned off here. The choice is kept in this browser only; nothing is sent.

4. What is not yet known

  • No one who relies on NVDA, VoiceOver or TalkBack has walked the site yet. Automated checks catch much, not all.
  • On 3 November 2026 the authors ran one automated NVDA pass: NVDA 2026.3 with Chrome on Windows, a script pressing the keys while NVDA's speech was written to a log, which was then read. It is not a test by people who use a screen reader, and it left out VoiceOver, TalkBack, Safari, Firefox, phones and every other screen reader. It went through the home, Features, Pricing, Download and Try the sample pages by keyboard (the controls in order, the headings, the landmarks and a read-through of each) and the Changelog's open-and-close. It found two things on other pages that are fixed: the Download page said nothing aloud when a release link was pressed, and Pricing's table read every group name as a column header. It also found three things on the home page, fixed since but not yet heard by NVDA again: the three "What would leave" buttons shared one name (each now names its connection), the questions under "Asked first" did not announce opening or closing (each held a heading, which took the place of its state; they no longer do), and the figures for a salary typed into "Your exact salary" were not spoken (they are now, politely, once you pause; the slider's always were).
  • The one-key shortcuts (/ for search) do not reach the page while NVDA is in browse mode, which keeps the key for itself; the Search link in the header does the same job, and the shortcut can be turned off above.
  • Try the sample shows the app's own screens, which keep the app's floor of 4.5:1 for all text rather than this site's 5:1 for small text.
  • This page is about the website. The Pocket app has checks of its own, and its accessibility is not described here.

5. Tell us what got in the way

Email support@mypocketfi.com with the page and what stopped you, and the setup you used if you can (browser, screen reader, zoom). Every email is read and answered by the founder.

6. Version history

3 November 2026: one automated NVDA pass by the authors (not a test by people who use a screen reader). The Download page now says aloud that a download is on its way, and Pricing's table no longer reads each group name as a column header. On the home page, each "What would leave" button names its connection, the questions under "Asked first" announce opening and closing, and a typed salary's figures are spoken; NVDA has not been run on these since.

1 November 2026: the table of every criterion at A and AA, each row with what checks it; the / key can be turned off; a control that takes focus is no longer left under the sticky header or a phone's download bar.
