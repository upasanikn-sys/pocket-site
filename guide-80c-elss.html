80C and ELSS within the ₹1.5 lakh limit

Category
Tax · FY 2026-27
By
Pocket
Reviewed
13 Oct 2026
Read
5 min
In short: section 80C deducts up to ₹1,50,000 a year in the old regime, across EPF, PPF, ELSS, life insurance and the rest together. Your EPF share often uses much of it before anything else: on a basic of ₹50,000 a month it is ₹72,000 a year. In the example below, ₹1,14,000 is already used, leaving ₹36,000 of room, and the full limit lowers the old regime's tax on a ₹15,00,000 salary by ₹46,800.

What counts, and the one limit

  • Your EPF share (12% of basic, from the payslip) and voluntary PF.
  • PPF and Sukanya Samriddhi deposits.
  • ELSS mutual funds (equity-linked savings schemes), locked in for three years from each investment.
  • Life insurance premiums, tax-saving fixed deposits of five years, NSC and the Senior Citizens' Savings Scheme.
  • Home-loan principal and children's tuition fees.

All of these share one limit of ₹1,50,000 a year. Anything above it deducts nothing more. NPS has a separate ₹50,000 of its own under 80CCD(1B); see NPS and 80CCD(1B).

How much is already used

  1. EPF, 12% of ₹50,000 × 12₹72,000
  2. PPF₹30,000
  3. Life insurance premium₹12,000
  4. Used₹1,14,000
  5. Room left in ₹1,50,000₹36,000

The room left inside the limit is ₹36,000. An ELSS SIP of ₹3,000 a month for twelve months would come to ₹36,000.

What each rupee is worth

The old regime's tax on a salary of ₹15,00,000, with no 80C, with the ₹1,14,000 already used, with the full limit, and with more than the limit:

80C put in80C deductedOld regime taxLower by
None₹0₹2,57,400₹0
₹1,14,000₹1,14,000₹2,21,832₹35,568
₹1,50,000₹1,50,000₹2,10,600₹46,800
₹2,00,000₹1,50,000₹2,10,600₹46,800

Here each rupee of 80C lowers the tax by 31.2 paise (30% plus 4% cess), because the income it comes off is taxed at 30%. On a smaller salary it comes off income taxed at 20% or 5%, so it is worth less. The last ₹36,000 of room is worth ₹11,232. Above ₹1,50,000 nothing changes. On the same salary the new regime, which allows no 80C, comes to ₹97,500. The old vs new regime guide works through where the old regime's deductions start to cost less.

One amount, two limits

How a year's amount meets 80C's ₹1,50,000 and 80CCD(1B)'s separate ₹50,000, which only NPS can use, for someone with nothing else under 80C (Pocket's engine, tax_india):

A yearUnder 80CUnder 80CCD(1B)Under neither
₹1,00,000₹1,00,000₹0₹0
₹2,00,000₹1,50,000₹50,000₹0
₹3,00,000₹1,50,000₹50,000₹1,00,000

Of ₹3,00,000 a year, ₹1,50,000 can count under 80C and ₹50,000 under 80CCD(1B); the remaining ₹1,00,000 counts under neither.

ELSS, in particular

  • Lock-in: three years from each investment, the shortest of the 80C options. A SIP's instalments unlock one by one, three years after each date.
  • Tax on the gains: an ELSS is an equity fund, and every ELSS unit is held over twelve months. A gain on it is a long-term capital gain: 12.5% on what is above ₹1,25,000 in the year. See the ₹1.25 lakh LTCG room.
  • No fixed return: it is invested in shares, so its value goes down as well as up.

Questions

What is the 80C limit for FY 2026-27?

₹1,50,000 a year, in the old regime only, across everything that counts under it: your EPF share, PPF, ELSS, life insurance premiums, home-loan principal, children's tuition fees and the rest.

Does my EPF count towards 80C?

Your own share does: 12% of a basic of ₹50,000 a month is ₹72,000 a year, already inside the limit before anything else is added.

How long is ELSS locked in?

Three years from the date each investment is made, so each SIP instalment is free three years after its own date.

Does 80C lower tax in the new regime?

No. The new regime allows no 80C deduction. On a salary of ₹15,00,000 its tax is ₹97,500.
