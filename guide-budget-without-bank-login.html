A budget app that never asks for your bank login

A guide · 11 October 2026
In short: to see where money goes, a budget needs your bank's transactions, not your bank password. Your bank's statement, downloaded as a CSV file, has every transaction. Pocket reads it on your own device, files salary as income, suggests a category for each spend and says where the suggestion came from, and keeps nothing anywhere but your device.

Why not a login

Apps that connect to a bank need access to the account, through a login or an account aggregator consent, and the transactions usually sit on their servers too. A statement file needs neither: you download it from net banking, as you would to print it, and the file never leaves your computer.

Getting the statement

In net banking, the account statement can be downloaded for any period, usually as PDF, Excel or CSV. Pocket reads the CSV. Where a bank offers only Excel, opening the file in a spreadsheet and saving it as CSV gives the same columns. Pocket knows the column layouts of HDFC, ICICI, SBI, Axis and Kotak statements, and finds the date, debit and credit columns in others. Dates are read as Indian banks write them, day first: 01/10/2026, 01/10/26, 2026-10-01 and 1 Oct 2026 all read the same.

03/10/26,NEFT-RENT OCTOBER,32000.00,,212544.00
05/10/26,ACH D- HDFC LTD EMI,11800.00,,200744.00
07/10/26,ELECTRICITY BESCOM,1870.00,,198874.00

And what Pocket does with them, run through its own importer:

  • Credits are income; the salary is filed as income straight away.
  • Each spend waits in the Inbox with a suggested category, and the reason for it: a word in the narration, how you filed the same payee before (a UPI ID counts as the payee), or that it looks like a transfer between your own accounts. Nothing is filed until you save it, and a rule you teach (for example, that entries containing "BESCOM" are Utilities) applies from then on.
  • The statement's closing balance, ₹1,98,874.00 on 7 Oct 2026, is offered as a bank account. It is added only if you say yes.

Importing the next month

A statement that overlaps the last one is safe to import: a transaction already in Pocket, with the same date, amount and description, is skipped. Importing this example a second time adds 0 entries. Pocket backs up its database before it adds anything, and a line it cannot read is listed with the reason, never guessed.

From transactions to a budget

Once spending is filed, Pocket shows each month's income against spending, by category; a budget for each category, or the 50/30/20 split of needs, wants and savings; and how many months your cash would last. The working behind each total opens to show its entries.
