An emergency fund in months, worked through

Category
Planning
By
Pocket
Reviewed
14 Oct 2026
Read
5 min
In short: an emergency fund is counted in months of essential spending. On ₹50,000 a month, Pocket's target of 6 months is ₹3,00,000. With ₹1,20,000 set aside, the fund covers 2.4 months and is 40% of the way; adding ₹15,000 a month, it is full in 12 months. The target is worked from spending, so when spending rises the target does. These are figures from stated assumptions, not predictions.

The target

Essential spending is what has to be paid whatever the income: rent or EMI, food, utilities, insurance premiums, school fees. On ₹50,000 a month:

  • The target: ₹50,000 × 6 months = ₹3,00,000.
  • Set aside today: ₹1,20,000, which is 40% of the target.
  • The runway: ₹1,20,000 ÷ ₹50,000 = 2.4 months of essential spending with no income at all.
  • Left to set aside: ₹3,00,000 − ₹1,20,000 = ₹1,80,000, which at ₹15,000 a month takes ₹1,80,000 ÷ ₹15,000 = 12 months. A part-month is counted up, as a savings goal counts, because the fund is full in the month the last rupee lands.

When the largest income stops

In a household with two incomes, ₹80,000 and ₹40,000 a month, the runway is not the only question: what the other income covers is.

If the larger income stops, ₹40,000 a month remains against ₹50,000 of essential spending. That leaves ₹10,000 a month to be paid from the fund, so the fund pays them for 12 months rather than 2.4. With one income of ₹80,000 and no other, it stops everything: the whole ₹50,000 a month comes from the fund, which is the 2.4 months above.

Prices move the target

A target set today is a sum in today's rupees.

At 6% inflation, ₹50,000 a month of essential spending is ₹66,911 a month in 5 years, and 6 months of that is ₹4,01,468, not ₹3,00,000. A fund that stays at ₹3,00,000 for 5 years covers 4.5 months of that, not 6.

What these figures assume

  • Essential spending that is known and steady. The runway divides by one month's figure.
  • The fund is not invested for growth. It is money to be had at once, so no return is added.
  • A fixed amount set aside each month, from the month after today, with no withdrawal.

How Pocket works it out

The figures on this page are Pocket's own, from its planning engine when the page was built.

They are the target, the progress and the months to full from the same code as the Goals page's emergency-fund card. The runway comes from the same code as the Protection page. There the runway is your liquid savings over your average month's spending: the last three finished months, or the figure typed in Settings. The Goals page's card shows what is set aside against its target and, at what is added each month, the month it is full.

Questions

How many months should an emergency fund cover?

Pocket's target is 6 months of essential spending, which on ₹50,000 a month is ₹3,00,000. Other counts are common, and the table above shows 3, 9 and 12 months; the count a household uses depends on how steady its income is and how many earn.

What counts as the fund?

Money that can be had at once and does not move in value: savings and bank accounts, cash and liquid funds. Where Pocket holds your accounts, the fund is what is in the accounts it counts as cash and bank, not a figure typed in.

What is the runway?

The months the fund pays your essential spending with no income: ₹1,20,000 ÷ ₹50,000 = 2.4 months.
