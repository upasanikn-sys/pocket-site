The SIP a goal needs, worked through

Category
Planning
By
Pocket
Reviewed
14 Oct 2026
Read
4 min
In short: a goal that costs ₹25,00,000 today costs ₹50,30,491 in 12 years at 6% a year inflation. With ₹3,00,000 already set aside, growing at 10% a year, the SIP that reaches it is ₹15,127 a month, counted at the start of each month: ₹21,78,289 paid in over 144 months. Sized to today's ₹25,00,000 instead, the SIP would be ₹5,766 a month, and would reach ₹25,00,000 in 12 years, which is ₹12,42,423 in today's rupees. These are projections under stated assumptions, not predictions.

The goal

A goal that costs ₹25,00,000 in today's rupees and falls due in 12 years, with ₹3,00,000 set aside for it.

  1. Its cost then. ₹25,00,000 × (1 + 6%)12 = ₹50,30,491.
  2. What is set aside, grown. ₹3,00,000 × (1 + 10%)12 = ₹9,41,529 in 12 years.
  3. What is left for the SIP. ₹50,30,491 − ₹9,41,529 = ₹40,88,963.
  4. What 144 SIPs of ₹1 grow to. At 0.7974% a month (10% a year compounded), each counted at the start of its month, ₹1 a month for 144 months grows to ₹270.31.
  5. The SIP. ₹40,88,963 ÷ ₹270.31 = ₹15,127 a month.

Checked the other way, ₹15,127 a month for 144 months at 10% comes to ₹40,88,963, and with the ₹9,41,529 that the money set aside has become, ₹50,30,491: the target.

Why the cost is grown first

A target is a sum of rupees on the day the goal falls due.

A cost known in today's rupees has to be grown with inflation to that day before it is compared with what a SIP builds. Left in today's rupees, the SIP comes to ₹5,766 a month, ₹9,361 less than the ₹15,127 the goal needs. It builds ₹25,00,000 with the money set aside, which is ₹12,42,423 in today's rupees and ₹25,30,491 short of the goal's cost then.

What the projection assumes

  • One return, 10% a year, every year, with nothing taken out before the goal.
  • One inflation rate, 6%, for the goal's cost. A cost that rises faster than 6% needs a higher target.
  • A flat SIP, the same amount every month. A SIP that steps up each year can start lower; the step-up guide works that through.

How Pocket works it out

The figures on this page are Pocket's own, from its goals engine when the page was built.

The Goals page checks a goal the same way: the target as typed, the money tracked against it, the SIP that reaches it, and what it is short by. It takes the target as a sum on the goal's date, so a cost known today is grown with inflation first, as above.

Questions

How is the SIP a goal needs worked out?

The goal's cost is grown with inflation to the year it falls due. The money already set aside is grown at the assumed return. What is left is divided by what a SIP of ₹1 a month, counted at the start of each month, would have grown to by then.

Why is the target higher than what the goal costs today?

Costs rise: at 6% a year, something that costs ₹25,00,000 today costs ₹50,30,491 in 12 years. A SIP sized to ₹25,00,000 would reach ₹25,00,000 in 12 years, which by then buys less than the goal does today.

What if the return is lower than the assumption?

The SIP needed is higher: ₹18,002 a month at 8% instead of ₹15,127 at 10%. The assumption is labelled because returns are not assured.
