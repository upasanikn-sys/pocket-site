The ₹1.25 lakh LTCG room, worked through

Category
Investing · FY 2026-27
By
Pocket
Reviewed
13 Oct 2026
Read
4 min
In short: each financial year the first ₹1,25,000 of long-term capital gains (LTCG) on listed shares and equity funds is not taxed. Gains already booked in the year use it up; what is left is the year's room, and it does not carry over. After ₹40,000 of equity long-term gains, ₹85,000 of room is left in FY 2026-27.

The rule

A long-term capital gain is taxed at 12.5% on what is above ₹1,25,000 in the financial year, plus 4% cess.

That is for listed shares, and for units of funds and ETFs that hold Indian equity. The year runs from 1 April to 31 March. The ₹1,25,000 is the year's total across every sale, not each sale's. It applies in both regimes, and a sale's gain counts in the year of the sale.

Up to ₹1,25,000 of gains in the year, the tax is nil; above it, each rupee is taxed at 13 paise (12.5% plus cess).

What is long-term in a holding now

A fund bought in two lots: 400 units on 3 Mar 2025 at ₹250 and 200 units on 1 Jan 2026 at ₹300. At a price of ₹330 on 12 Oct 2026:

  • Long-term now: the March 2025 units, held over 12 months, carry a gain of ₹32,000.
  • Short-term now: the January 2026 units carry ₹6,000; they turn long-term on 2 Jan 2027.

Because units are sold oldest first, a sale of up to 400 of these units would draw only on the long-term lot. A gain booked inside the room is taxed at nil. Selling units and buying the same fund back, sometimes called harvesting, books the gain inside the year's room and starts the new units' cost at the price paid. It also starts a new holding period. Pocket reports each lot's gain and term; it does not suggest a sale.

Questions

How much equity long-term gain is tax-free in FY 2026-27?

The first ₹1,25,000 of long-term gains on listed shares and equity funds in the financial year; what is above it is taxed at 12.5%, plus cess.

Does unused room carry over to the next year?

No. The ₹1,25,000 is for each financial year; what is not used by 31 March is gone.

Does it cover gold ETFs or debt funds?

No. It is for equity: listed shares, and funds and ETFs that hold Indian equity. A gold ETF's long-term gain is taxed at 12.5% from the first rupee.

How much room is left after ₹40,000 of gains?

₹1,25,000 − ₹40,000 = ₹85,000.
