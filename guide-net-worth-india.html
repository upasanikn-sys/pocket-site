How to work out your net worth in India

Category
Planning
By
Pocket
Reviewed
12 Oct 2026
Read
5 min
In short: net worth is what you own minus what you owe. In India that means adding up bank balances and fixed deposits, shares and mutual funds at today's prices, EPF, PPF and NPS, and gold or property at a value you choose, then taking away every loan and card balance. The total is only as good as its oldest figure, so each figure needs a date.

What counts

A net worth is one number, but it is built from five kinds of things. Pocket groups them the same way:

  • Cash & bank: savings and salary accounts, cash, and fixed deposits.
  • Investments: shares, ETFs, mutual funds, Sovereign Gold Bonds and crypto, each at its price today (in rupees, for anything priced in dollars).
  • Retirement: EPF, PPF and NPS. They are locked in for years, but they are yours, so they count.
  • Physical & other: gold jewellery, property, a vehicle, a share of a business, at the value you put on them.
  • Liabilities: home, car and personal loans at their outstanding balance, and credit cards at what is owed today.

Net worth is the first four added together, minus the fifth.

Where each figure comes from

  • Bank accounts: the balance in net banking or the last statement.
  • Mutual funds: a CAMS or KFintech Consolidated Account Statement (CAS) lists every fund across fund houses, with units and NAV; see tracking mutual funds from a CAS.
  • Shares and ETFs: the holdings page at your broker, at today's price.
  • EPF: the member passbook on the EPFO portal or in the UMANG app.
  • PPF: the passbook or statement from the bank or post office that holds it.
  • NPS: the statement from your NPS record-keeping agency.
  • Loans: the outstanding principal on the lender's statement, not the original loan amount.

The total

₹9,16,037.36 + ₹14,41,000.00 − ₹4,38,400.00 = ₹19,18,637.36.

Three things in this example are easy to get wrong by hand. The Apple shares are priced in dollars, so each line multiplies by the exchange rate as well. The car loan counts at what is still owed, not at what was borrowed. And the Public Provident Fund figure is from 30 May 2026: the total is only as current as that.

Mistakes that move the number

  • Counting something twice: a mutual fund listed both as a holding and as an account. Pocket counts an account of an investment kind once, from the holdings.
  • The house without the loan: a home at its market value, with its home loan left out, overstates net worth by the whole loan.
  • Cost instead of value: what a fund cost is not what it is worth today.
  • Old figures: a PPF balance from last year, or a gold value from when it was bought. Pocket shows each account's date beside it, so an old one is visible.

Questions

What counts towards net worth in India?

Cash and bank balances and fixed deposits; shares, ETFs and mutual funds at today's price; EPF, PPF and NPS; gold, property and other things at a value you choose; minus every loan and card balance.

Does a loan count at what was borrowed?

No, at what is still owed: the outstanding principal on the lender's statement. In the worked example the liabilities come to ₹4,38,400.00.

Do EPF and PPF count when they are locked in?

Yes. They are yours, so they count, at the balance on the passbook and with its date beside it.
