NPS and 80CCD(1B), worked through

Category
Retirement · FY 2026-27
By
Pocket
Reviewed
13 Oct 2026
Read
4 min
In short: section 80CCD(1B) deducts up to ₹50,000 a year of your own National Pension System (NPS) contributions, in the old regime, on top of 80C's ₹1,50,000. On a salary of ₹18,00,000 with 80C already full, it lowers the year's tax by ₹15,600. ₹5,000 a month in NPS for 25 years comes to ₹66,89,452 at an assumed 10% a year.

The three NPS deductions

  • 80CCD(1): your own contribution, inside 80C's ₹1,50,000 limit.
  • 80CCD(1B): ₹50,000 more of your own contribution, beyond 80C. Old regime only.
  • 80CCD(2): your employer's contribution, up to 14% of basic and dearness allowance in the new regime (10% in the old). The only one the new regime allows. Pocket does not work it out.

The deduction is for contributions to an NPS Tier I account. A Tier II account has no deduction (except the central government's own scheme for its employees) and no lock-in.

The ₹50,000 comes off income taxed at 30%, so the old regime's tax is ₹15,600 lower. On the same salary the new regime, which allows no 80CCD(1B), comes to ₹1,50,800. The old vs new regime guide works through where the old regime's deductions start to cost less.

At 60

The corpus is locked in until 60.

At exit, part can be taken as a lump sum and the rest buys an annuity, a pension whose income is taxed as it is paid. PFRDA's exit rules set the shares. The Income-tax Act sets how much of the lump sum is tax-free. Pocket's projection stops at the corpus.

Questions

How much does 80CCD(1B) deduct?

Up to ₹50,000 a year of your own NPS contributions, in the old regime only, on top of 80C's ₹1,50,000.

Is 80CCD(1B) allowed in the new regime?

No. The new regime allows only the employer's contribution to NPS, under 80CCD(2), up to 14% of basic and dearness allowance.

What does the 80CCD(1B) deduction save on ₹18,00,000?

In the old regime, with 80C already full, ₹15,600: ₹50,000 that would have been taxed at 30%, plus cess.

What could ₹5,000 a month in NPS grow to in 25 years?

₹66,89,452 at 10% a year, an assumption and not a promise; ₹47,86,833 at 8% and ₹94,88,175 at 12%.
