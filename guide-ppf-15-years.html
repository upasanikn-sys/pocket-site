PPF over 15 years, worked through

Category
Retirement
By
Pocket
Reviewed
14 Oct 2026
Read
6 min
In short: the Public Provident Fund takes ₹500 to ₹1,50,000 a financial year for 15 years, at an interest rate the government sets each quarter: 7.1% for October to December 2026, as it has been since 1 April 2020. ₹1,50,000 paid in by 5 April each year comes to ₹40,68,209 after 15 years at 7.1%, of which ₹18,18,209 is interest, all of it tax-free. These are illustrations at today's rate, not predictions.

The rules

  • Deposits: at least ₹500 and at most ₹1,50,000 in each financial year (April to March), in multiples of ₹50.
  • Interest: worked out each month on the lowest balance between the close of the 5th and the end of the month, and added once a year, on 31 March. Money in by the 5th earns that month's interest; money in on the 6th does not.
  • Term: the account matures after fifteen whole financial years from the end of the year it was opened in. It can then be extended in blocks of five years, with deposits or without.
  • Rate: set by the Ministry of Finance for each quarter. It has been 7.1% since 1 April 2020; it was 7.9% before that.
  • Tax: deposits count toward the old regime's ₹1,50,000 limit under 80C; the interest and the maturity amount are tax-free.

By year 15, ₹22,50,000 has gone in and the balance is ₹40,68,209. The interest grows each year because it is added to the balance and earns interest itself. The last five years add ₹18,38,085, more than the whole balance after the first 8 years, ₹16,54,185.

The first two years, worked through

  • Year 1: ₹1,50,000 × (1 + 7.1%) = ₹1,60,650.
  • Year 2: (₹1,60,650 + ₹1,50,000) × (1 + 7.1%) = ₹3,32,706.
  • Each year after: the balance plus that year's deposit, × (1 + 7.1%).

Worked out by the month, as the scheme does, the result is the same for money that is in by 5 April. It sits in the account for all twelve months, and the year's interest is added on 31 March.

When it matures, and after

The fifteen years count from the end of the financial year the account is opened in.

An account opened in the first days of April 2026, in the financial year 2026-27, matures on 1 April 2042, after the years 2027-28 to 2041-42. It takes deposits in sixteen financial years, the year it was opened and fifteen more. With ₹1,50,000 in by 5 April in each of the sixteen, it comes to ₹45,17,702 at 7.1%.

At maturity the account can be closed, or extended for five years at a time. With ₹1,50,000 a year continuing:

How Pocket works it out

The figures on this page are Pocket's own, from its planning engine's PPF projection when the page was built.

Each year's deposit is at the start of the year, and the year's interest is on the balance. In the app, your PPF balance goes in Accounts & assets under Retirement, with the date it stands at. There it counts in your net worth and in the corpus the Goals page projects your retirement from.

Questions

How much can go into a PPF account in a year?

At least ₹500 and at most ₹1,50,000 in a financial year, in multiples of ₹50, in one go or in several deposits. A year with less than ₹500 leaves the account discontinued until it is revived with a fee of ₹50 and ₹500 for each year missed.

When is PPF interest added?

It is worked out for each month on the lowest balance between the close of the 5th and the end of the month. It is added to the account once a year, at the end of the financial year. A deposit made after the 5th of a month earns nothing for that month.

What does ₹1,50,000 a year come to after 15 years?

₹40,68,209 at 7.1% a year, if each year's deposit is in by 5 April and the rate stays 7.1%: ₹22,50,000 paid in and ₹18,18,209 of interest. The rate is set by the government every quarter, so the real figure moves with it.

Is PPF interest taxed?

No. The interest and the amount at maturity are tax-free, and deposits count toward the old regime's ₹1,50,000 limit under 80C; the new regime has no 80C deduction.
