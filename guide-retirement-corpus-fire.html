How much you need to retire, and the year you reach it

Category
Retirement
By

Reviewed

Read

In short: Pocket works the need as a year's spending, grown with inflation to the day of retirement and divided by a withdrawal rate. On ₹6,00,000 a year today (₹50,000 a month), 25 years to go, 6% inflation and a 4% withdrawal rate, the need is ₹25,75,122 a year then and a corpus of ₹6,43,78,061, which is ₹1,50,00,000 in today's rupees: 25 times a year's spending now. With ₹10,00,000 invested and a ₹20,000 a month SIP stepping up 10% a year, all at 10% a year, the corpus first covers the need in year 24. These are projections under stated assumptions, not predictions.

The need

  1. A year's spending today: ₹6,00,000.
  2. Grown with inflation for 25 years at 6%: ₹6,00,000 × (1 + 6%)25 = ₹25,75,122 a year.
  3. Divided by the withdrawal rate: ₹25,75,122 ÷ 4% = ₹6,43,78,061.

That corpus is ₹1,50,00,000 in today's rupees, 25 times a year's spending now. The need is grown with inflation before it is set beside a corpus, so that both are in the rupees of the same year.

The corpus first reaches the need in year 24: ₹6,41,79,583 against ₹6,07,34,020. This is the financial-independence year: 24 years from now, 1 year before the 25 years the plan was made for. The need grows too, at 6% a year, so the corpus is compared with the need of that year, not today's.

What the projection assumes

  • One return, 10% a year, on the corpus until the year it is set beside the need, and a SIP paid each month.
  • One inflation rate, 6%, for every year, and a need that is spending alone: no tax, no change in what is spent.
  • A rule of thumb, not a guarantee. A withdrawal rate of 4% says what share of the corpus is taken in the first year; markets and spending in a particular retirement can differ from the average.

How Pocket works it out

The figures on this page are Pocket's own, from its planning engine when the page was built.

They are the retirement need, the projection of the corpus with a step-up SIP, and the first year it is covered. The Goals page works the same for your annual expenses, years to retirement and SIP, and shows that year as Financial independence.

Questions

How much do I need to retire in India?

Pocket's rule: a year's spending, grown at 6% a year to the day of retirement, divided by a withdrawal rate of 4%. On ₹6,00,000 a year today and 25 years to go, that is ₹25,75,122 a year then and a corpus of ₹6,43,78,061. That is ₹1,50,00,000 in today's rupees: 25 times a year's spending today.

What is the withdrawal rate?

The share of the corpus taken out in the first year of retirement, and then increased with inflation. 4% is a rule of thumb for the 'safe-withdrawal' rate, not a guarantee. The table above shows what the corpus becomes at other rates.

What is the financial-independence year?

The first year in which the corpus, projected with the SIP and its step-up, reaches the corpus that year's spending needs. With the SIP and the need both growing, it can come before the retirement date the plan was made for.
