Step-up vs flat SIP, worked through

Category
Investing
By
Pocket
Reviewed
14 Oct 2026
Read
4 min
In short: a flat SIP pays the same amount every month; a step-up SIP raises it by a fixed percentage each year. Starting at ₹10,000 a month for 20 years at 10% a year, a flat SIP comes to ₹72,39,867 and a 10% step-up SIP to ₹1,54,61,674, 2.1 times as much, because it also pays in more: ₹68,73,000 against ₹24,00,000. By year 20 the step-up SIP is ₹61,159 a month. These are projections under stated assumptions, not predictions.

The two SIPs

  • Flat: ₹10,000 on the 1st of every month for 20 years.
  • Step-up: ₹10,000 a month in the first year, then 10% more each year: ₹11,000 in the second, ₹12,100 in the third, and so on.

Both earn 10% a year, which Pocket works as 0.7974% a month (10% compounded: 1.101/12 − 1), and each instalment is counted at the start of its month. The month-by-month working is below.

The step-up SIP's corpus after 20 years is 2.1 times the flat one's, with 2.9 times as much paid in: ₹68,73,000 against ₹24,00,000. The rupees paid in later have grown for fewer years, which is why the corpus rises by less than the amount paid in.

To reach the ₹1,54,61,674 that the 10% step-up gives, a flat SIP would have to be ₹21,356 a month from the first month (2.1 times ₹10,000).

The first two months, worked through

  • Month 1: ₹10,000 × (1 + 0.7974%) = ₹10,079.74.
  • Month 2: (₹10,079.74 + ₹10,000) × (1 + 0.7974%) = ₹20,239.86.
  • Each month after: (the balance + that month's SIP) × (1 + 0.7974%); the SIP rises by 10% every twelfth month.

In today's money

Prices rise too.

At 6% a year, ₹1,54,61,674 20 years from now buys what ₹48,21,023 buys today. The projection is in the rupees of the year it is reached, and so is the SIP paid in: ₹61,159 a month in year 20 is ₹19,070 in today's money.

What the projection assumes

  • One return, 10% a year, every year, after costs: no bad years, and nothing taken out.
  • A step-up that is paid. The step-up SIP needs ₹61,159 a month by year 20; the projection takes it that income has risen to pay it.
  • The first SIP counted at the start of month 1. A SIP that starts mid-month, or is skipped, changes the figures.

Questions

What is a step-up SIP?

A SIP whose amount is raised by a fixed percentage once a year, so that it keeps pace with pay rises. A 10% step-up on ₹10,000 a month makes it ₹11,000 a month in the second year, ₹12,100 in the third, and so on.

Is a SIP counted at the start or the end of the month?

Pocket counts each SIP at the start of its month, as a SIP debit is, so the instalment earns that month's return. A SIP counted at the end of each month would come to a little less.

Does 10% a year mean a fund will return 10%?

No. 10% is the planning assumption the projection uses for every year; mutual-fund returns are not assured and differ from year to year. The page states it so the figures can be redone at another rate.
