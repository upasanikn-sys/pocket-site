Glossary

  • 80C Section 80C. Up to ₹1,50,000 a year of EPF, PPF, ELSS, life insurance premiums and some other savings comes off your income in the old regime. The new regime allows none.
  • 80D Section 80D. Health insurance premiums come off your income in the old regime: up to ₹25,000 for you, your spouse and children, and ₹25,000 more for your parents. Each limit is ₹50,000 where someone covered is 60 or over.
  • Advance tax Tax on income TDS does not cover, paid in four instalments by 15 June, 15 September, 15 December and 15 March: 15%, 45%, 75% and all of it. It applies only when the year's tax less TDS reaches ₹10,000.
  • Avalanche Paying off loans, highest rate first. Pay every debt's minimum and put the rest of the month's budget on the debt with the highest rate, rolling a cleared debt's payment on to the next.
  • CAS Consolidated Account Statement. A password-protected PDF from CAMS or KFintech that lists every mutual fund you hold across fund houses. The detailed one has every transaction.
  • Cess Health and education cess. 4% of the tax and surcharge, in both regimes.
  • CTC Cost to company. Everything an employer spends on you, including its PF contribution and gratuity, which are not paid to you each month.
  • ELSS Equity-linked savings scheme. An equity mutual fund whose units count toward the 80C limit and are locked in for three years from each investment. A gain on it is a long-term capital gain: 12.5% on what is above ₹1,25,000 in the year.
  • Emergency fund Money set aside for essential spending, counted in months of it. Pocket's target is 6 months.
  • EPF Employees' Provident Fund. 12% of basic pay from you and 12% from your employer go in each month. The balance earns the interest rate EPFO declares each year.
  • Financial-independence year The first year the corpus, projected with the SIP and its step-up, reaches the corpus that year's spending needs. With the SIP and the need both growing, it can come before the planned retirement date.
  • HRA House rent allowance. The part of your pay meant for rent. In the old regime the exempt part is the least of three figures: the HRA received, the rent paid less 10% of basic, and 50% or 40% of basic by city.
  • LTCG Long-term capital gains. The gain on listed shares or equity funds held for more than 12 months. The first ₹1,25,000 a year is tax-free. The rest is taxed at 12.5%, plus cess.
  • Marginal relief A limit on the extra tax just above a threshold: at most the income above it. Above the new regime's ₹12,00,000 rebate limit the tax is at most the income above ₹12,00,000. Surcharge thresholds work the same way.
  • NAV Net asset value. The value of one unit of a mutual fund on a day. A statement's closing units at its NAV come to the market value it states.
  • Net worth What you own minus what you owe: bank balances, fixed deposits, shares and mutual funds at today's prices, EPF, PPF, NPS, and gold or property at a value you choose, less loans and card balances. Each figure needs a date.
  • NPS National Pension System. A retirement account you pay into until retirement. Up to ₹50,000 a year of your own contribution, beyond 80C, comes off your income in the old regime under 80CCD(1B).
  • PPF Public Provident Fund. A 15-year savings account at a bank or post office. It takes ₹500 to ₹1,50,000 a financial year, at an interest rate the government sets each quarter.
  • Rebate Rebate under section 87A. No tax at all on taxable income up to ₹12,00,000 in the new regime, and up to ₹5,00,000 in the old. With the standard deduction, a salary of up to ₹12,75,000 pays no tax in the new regime.
  • Runway The months the emergency fund pays your essential spending with no income: the fund divided by a month's essential spending.
  • SIP Systematic investment plan. A fixed amount put into a mutual fund on the same day each month.
  • Snowball Paying off loans, smallest balance first. Pay every debt's minimum and put the rest of the month's budget on the debt with the smallest balance, rolling a cleared debt's payment on to the next.
  • Standard deduction A flat amount taken off a salary before tax: ₹75,000 in the new regime, ₹50,000 in the old.
  • Step-up SIP A SIP raised by a fixed percentage once a year to keep pace with pay rises. A 10% step-up on ₹10,000 a month makes it ₹11,000 a month in the second year and ₹12,100 in the third.
  • Surcharge An addition to the tax on taxable income above ₹50 lakh: 10% of the tax, 15% above ₹1 crore, 25% above ₹2 crore, in both regimes; above ₹5 crore, 37% in the old regime and 25% in the new.
  • TDS Tax deducted at source. Income tax taken off your pay by your employer each month: a twelfth of the year's tax on the salary. It counts toward the year's tax, so only what TDS does not cover is left to pay.
  • Withdrawal rate The share of the corpus taken out in the first year of retirement, and then increased with inflation. 4% is a rule of thumb for the 'safe-withdrawal' rate, not a guarantee.
  • XIRR Extended internal rate of return. The annualised, money-weighted rate of return on a portfolio, counting each dated purchase on its own date. Pocket shows it once money has been invested for about six months.
