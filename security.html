What is not finished yet.

Read this before relying on Pocket.

  • Database encryption at rest is yours to turn on. On Windows, turn it on in Settings, Privacy & security (SQLCipher, AES-256, with a recovery passphrase only you hold). On a Mac or Linux it does not yet: use FileVault or full-disk encryption. Android always encrypts.
  • The builds are not code-signed yet. Windows and macOS warn on first run. Check the file against its SHA-256 on the Download page first.
  • No independent security audit has been done. These pages are Pocket's own account, checked against the source.
  • While Pocket runs, its database is open to it, and to anything else running as you. Malware on the computer defeats every protection here.

What is encrypted, and how.

Two things always, one when you turn it on. For the rest, use BitLocker, FileVault or full-disk encryption.

WhatEncryptedHow
Document vault ProAlwaysAES-256-GCM, a fresh key per file from your passphrase through scrypt; the passphrase is never stored
Encrypted backup ProAlwaysThe same scheme, under your passphrase, before the copy reaches the folder
Database, WindowsWhen you turn it onSQLCipher 4 (AES-256); its key locked to your Windows account (DPAPI) and kept under your recovery passphrase
Database, macOS and LinuxNot yetProtected by your account and your disk encryption
Database, AndroidAlwaysSQLCipher, with a random key in the phone's secure storage
App lock PINNever storedOnly a scrypt hash of it, checked by Pocket on your computer
